# Examination and Assessment Regulations

## 1. Eligibility

A student is eligible to sit the final examination of a module only if they are registered for the module, have paid all fees and meet the minimum attendance requirement of 80%. Students who are not eligible will not be admitted to the examination hall.

## 2. Conduct of Examinations

Students must arrive at least fifteen minutes before the examination start time and present a valid student identity card. Bags, coats and electronic devices, including mobile phones and smart watches, must be left in the designated area outside the hall. Only approved non-programmable calculators may be used when the examination paper permits a calculator.

## 3. Late Arrival

Students who arrive up to 30 minutes after the start of an examination may be admitted but will not be given extra time. Students who arrive more than 30 minutes late will not be admitted and will be recorded as absent. No student may leave the hall during the first 30 minutes or the last 15 minutes of an examination.

## 4. Illness and Absence

A student who is ill on the day of an examination should not sit the paper. They must notify the Examinations Office before the examination starts and submit a mitigating circumstances application with a medical certificate within seven days. If the application is approved, the student may sit a deferred examination at the next opportunity without penalty. Students who sit an examination are considered fit to do so and cannot later claim illness for that paper.

## 5. Resits and Referrals

A student who fails a module may be offered a resit, also called a referral, in the failed component. The mark for a resit module is capped at the pass mark of 40%. Resit examinations for Semester 1 modules are held during the resit period, which starts on [[+200d]]. A student may resit a module only once; a second failure requires the student to repeat the module with attendance.

## 6. Academic Misconduct in Examinations

Any attempt to gain an unfair advantage, such as bringing unauthorised notes, copying from another student or communicating during the examination, is examination misconduct. Invigilators will confiscate unauthorised material and report the incident to the Academic Misconduct Panel. Penalties range from a mark of zero for the paper to exclusion from the university.

## 7. Grading and Classification

Module marks are converted to grades: 70% and above is grade A, 60–69% is grade B, 50–59% is grade C, 40–49% is grade D and below 40% is a fail. The final degree classification is based on the weighted average of the level 5 and level 6 modules: First Class for an average of 70% or above, Upper Second for 60–69%, Lower Second for 50–59% and Third Class for 40–49%.

## 8. Appeals

Students may appeal against an examination board decision only on the grounds of a procedural irregularity or mitigating circumstances that could not have been reported earlier. Disagreement with the academic judgement of examiners is not a valid ground for appeal. Appeals must be submitted in writing to the Academic Registrar within 14 days of the publication of results.
