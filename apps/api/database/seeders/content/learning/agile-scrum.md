# Agile and Scrum Fundamentals

Agile is a family of software development approaches based on the Agile Manifesto published in 2001. The manifesto values individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan. Scrum is the most widely used agile framework.

## Scrum roles

Scrum defines three accountabilities. The product owner is responsible for maximising the value of the product and manages the product backlog. The Scrum master is a servant leader who coaches the team, removes impediments and makes sure the Scrum process is understood. The developers are the self-organising professionals who build a usable increment every sprint.

## Scrum artefacts

The product backlog is an ordered list of everything that might be needed in the product. A user story describes a feature from the user's point of view, often written as "As a student, I want to see my deadlines so that I can plan my week". The sprint backlog is the set of backlog items selected for the current sprint together with the plan for delivering them. The increment is the sum of all completed backlog items, and it must meet the definition of done.

## Scrum events

A sprint is a fixed time-box, usually two weeks, in which the team creates a releasable increment. Sprint planning decides what can be delivered and how the work will be achieved. The daily scrum is a fifteen-minute meeting in which developers inspect progress toward the sprint goal and adapt their plan. The sprint review demonstrates the increment to stakeholders and collects feedback. The sprint retrospective is held at the end of each sprint so the team can improve its way of working.

## Estimation and velocity

Agile teams often estimate user stories with story points, which measure relative effort rather than hours. Planning poker is a consensus-based estimation technique in which team members reveal their estimates at the same time. Velocity is the number of story points a team completes in a sprint, and it is used to forecast how much work fits into future sprints. A burndown chart shows the remaining work in a sprint and helps the team see whether it is on track.

## Benefits and challenges

Scrum improves visibility, delivers value early and adapts well to changing requirements. However, it requires an engaged product owner, a disciplined team and a culture that accepts transparency. Common anti-patterns include treating the daily scrum as a status report to a manager, skipping retrospectives and allowing the sprint scope to change after planning.
